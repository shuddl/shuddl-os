## The 100-word pitch

SHUDDL is a freight operating system for regional carriers that runs on physical proof. When your driver completes a stop, the signature, photos, GPS, and timestamps are sealed into a tamper-evident record — and the invoice, with all evidence attached, is in your customer's inbox the same second. Detention claims carry their own proof. Disputes end with a link instead of an argument. Nothing gets re-typed into a TMS, a spreadsheet, or QuickBooks, because the money side is generated from what physically happened. It's built, it's running on our infrastructure today, and we're selecting a small group of Founding Carriers now.

## Naming rule — subjects vs. citations (clarified 2026-08-02, tick 8)

A brief in this program once said "no company name may appear at all," which is too blunt and produced an inconsistency between content pieces. The precise rule:

- **Banned — companies as *subjects*:** any tenant, customer, prospect, person, or incumbent vendor discussed as a subject of our argument. Say "carrier-identity platforms," never a brand. This is what REQ-167 protects: it keeps us from leaking who we work with or from picking public fights.
- **Allowed — publications and research bodies as *citations*:** a source's name and its URL, including trade outlets, when we are attributing a figure. This is normal attribution, it makes a claim checkable, and a reader who can verify us trusts us more. Research bodies and agencies (ATRI, OOIDA, TIA, Verisk CargoNet, FMCSA, FTC, BLS) are always allowed.

Applies to every content piece, the press kit, the calculator footnotes, and sequence copy. Where a figure's only available source is a vendor's own blog, prefer omitting the figure over citing the vendor — that is a sourcing-quality judgment, not a naming one.

## Hard don'ts (the over-claim firewall)
- Never "customers are using…" — we have zero tenants; say **"built and running on our infrastructure; entering Founding Carrier pilots."**
- Never name a tenant, person, customer, or incumbent vendor. Never imply endorsements.
- Never "AI-powered" as the lead — the market is in agent-washing fatigue (Gartner, Jun 2026); lead with proof-and-payment, mention automation as how, not what.
- Never promise the <5s number as production fact — it is a staging-measured mechanism until tenant-0 measures it live.
- Never platform/network/coordination-layer language in public — carrier value only, until tenant-0 is live.
